# Apex Cinematic Homepage Storyboard

**Status:** CANONICAL STORYBOARD / BUILD INPUT  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-04  
**Parent vision:** `apps/website/docs/cinematic-homepage-vision.md`  
**Scope:** V1 hero sequence — luxury backyard → run → jump → splash → underwater reveal  
**Owner:** Nick Sandoval

---

# 1. Objective

Build one scroll-controlled cinematic sequence that establishes the new Apex website direction before expanding the rest of the site.

The V1 hero must create a premium, emotionally memorable first impression while proving that the underlying scroll interaction is smooth, reversible, performant, and strong enough to carry the rest of the website.

The visitor should feel like they are controlling time inside a luxury brand film.

The emotional story is:

**anticipation → movement → joy → impact → immersion → craftsmanship**

The product story is:

**Apex creates the backyard → the family experiences it → the visitor enters the pool → the craftsmanship is revealed**

---

# 2. Canonical scene lock

Before any sequence generation, these elements must be locked and reused.

## Family

### Father
- age range: approximately late 30s to early 40s
- athletic but natural build
- warm, approachable appearance
- not model-perfect
- authentic family energy
- short practical hairstyle
- no visible logos
- neutral upscale swimwear

### Son A
- approximately 9–11 years old
- energetic
- distinct from Son B in height/body language
- swim trunks that visually separate him from the father and brother

### Son B
- approximately 6–8 years old
- slightly smaller
- playful body language
- distinct swimsuit color/pattern

The family must remain visually identical across the sequence.

No face drift, wardrobe changes, body proportion changes, or age changes.

---

# 3. Environment lock

## Location feel
Luxury West Texas / Lubbock backyard.

The architecture should feel believable for an upper-end custom home in the region rather than coastal California, Miami, or Scottsdale fantasy architecture.

## Pool
Premium custom pool with:
- elegant modern geometry
- tanning ledge
- integrated spa or visual spa connection
- premium coping
- refined tile
- strong underwater visibility
- subtle water feature opportunity
- architectural steps / ledges that can later be showcased underwater

The pool should look buildable by Apex.

Avoid impossible infinity edges, extreme cliffside settings, tropical vegetation, or resort-scale architecture that would feel disconnected from Apex's market.

## Backyard
- modern West Texas home
- premium but restrained materials
- tasteful outdoor living area
- realistic landscaping for the climate
- room for the three family members to run naturally toward the pool
- depth behind the pool for cinematic composition
- no clutter
- no visible neighbors if avoidable

## Time / lighting
Golden hour moving toward early evening.

Warm sun should rake across the scene and create dimensionality without blowing out the water surface.

The light direction must stay consistent throughout the sequence.

---

# 4. Camera language

The camera should feel cinematic and expensive, not like a drone reel or stock commercial.

## Visual lens language
Primary feel: 24–35 mm full-frame equivalent.

Use wider framing for the environment, then slowly move toward 35 mm visual compression near the hero jump.

## Camera behavior
- slow dolly / push-in
- subtle vertical lowering during acceleration
- slight lateral/orbital motion during the jump
- intentional drive toward the water during impact
- seamless passage through the splash
- controlled glide underwater

Avoid:
- handheld shake
- aggressive whip pans
- extreme orbiting
- fisheye distortion
- dramatic lens breathing
- abrupt cuts before splash

The user should perceive one continuous cinematic event.

---

# 5. Sequence architecture

Target initial source motion: **12–15 seconds**.

Target web sequence: approximately **120–160 meaningful frames** after optimization.

Target pinned scroll range for V1 prototype: approximately **350–450vh**.

Final timing should be tuned after the prototype.

---

# 6. Shot-by-shot storyboard

## SHOT 01 — Stillness / aspiration

**Narrative function:** Establish the dream.

**Approx. source time:** 0.0–1.5 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 0–10%  
**Camera:** Wide architectural composition, slightly above waterline, slow push forward.  
**Family:** Father and sons entering frame or already walking casually toward pool.  
**Pool:** Calm, reflective surface.  
**Motion:** Almost none at first. Very gentle environmental movement only.

### Composition
The pool should dominate the lower half of the frame.

The house and outdoor space create the luxury context.

The family is visible but not yet dominant.

### Copy
Primary opening option:

> **BUILD THE BACKYARD THEY'LL REMEMBER.**

Display for only the opening portion.

Fade before the run begins.

### Keyframe
**KF-001 — Canonical hero still**

This is the single most important generation frame because it establishes:
- family identity
- wardrobe
- house
- pool
- landscaping
- lighting
- material palette
- visual style

This keyframe must be approved before sequence generation.

---

## SHOT 02 — Anticipation

**Narrative function:** Turn a static luxury scene into a human moment.

**Approx. source time:** 1.5–3.0 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 10–23%  
**Camera:** Continued slow dolly forward.  
**Family:** Walking turns into playful urgency. Boys begin moving faster. Dad reacts and joins them.  
**Motion:** Natural acceleration rather than instantly sprinting.

### Direction
This should feel spontaneous.

The boys may glance toward one another or toward the water.

Dad is participating, not supervising.

No text.

### Keyframe
**KF-020 — Family acceleration**

Used to confirm:
- faces still match
- wardrobe matches
- pool and architecture remain unchanged
- body positions remain anatomically credible

---

## SHOT 03 — Run

**Narrative function:** Build energy.

**Approx. source time:** 3.0–5.0 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 23–40%  
**Camera:** Lower slightly and push closer. A tiny lateral shift creates depth.  
**Family:** All three running toward the pool edge.  
**Motion:** Faster, energetic, but not slapstick.

### Composition
Dad should become visually central.

The boys should remain readable on either side or staggered naturally.

The pool edge becomes a strong leading line.

No copy.

### Keyframe
**KF-040 — Final stride**

Capture the last full stride before takeoff.

This is important for making the jump feel physically connected to the run.

---

## SHOT 04 — Takeoff

**Narrative function:** Begin the signature moment.

**Approx. source time:** 5.0–6.0 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 40–50%  
**Camera:** Push slows. Slight sideways/orbital move begins.  
**Family:** Feet leave the edge in quick succession.  
**Motion:** Natural stagger acceptable; perfectly synchronized jumping may feel staged.

### Direction
The father should not perform an extreme trick.

Joy is more important than athletic spectacle.

The sons can have more playful silhouettes.

### Keyframe
**KF-055 — First airborne frame**

This establishes the transition into the slow-motion zone.

---

## SHOT 05 — Hero flight

**Narrative function:** Deliver the defining Apex website moment.

**Approx. source time:** 6.0–8.0 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 50–68%  
**Camera:** Apparent time slows significantly. Subtle 8–15° lateral/orbital movement adds dimensionality.  
**Family:** All three clearly airborne above the pool.  
**Motion:** Slow, controllable, visually readable.

### Composition
This is the money shot.

Preferred arrangement:
- father near visual center
- one son slightly forward/higher
- one son slightly trailing/lower
- enough separation between bodies to read all three silhouettes
- beautiful pool visible beneath them
- house/background still recognizable

Avoid awkward body intersections.

### Facial emotion
- authentic excitement
- smiling / laughing
- no exaggerated AI facial expressions
- no direct camera posing

### Optional copy
If imagery leaves sufficient negative space:

> **THIS IS WHY WE BUILD.**

This line should be subtle and may ultimately be removed.

### Keyframes
**KF-070 — Early hero flight**  
**KF-080 — Peak hero flight**  
**KF-090 — Descent begins**

These three are essential continuity checkpoints.

**KF-080** should be good enough to use independently in campaigns, social, or a static fallback.

---

## SHOT 06 — Descent

**Narrative function:** Build anticipation immediately before impact.

**Approx. source time:** 8.0–8.8 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 68–76%  
**Camera:** Begins driving toward the water with them.  
**Family:** Descending naturally.  
**Water:** Reflection and perspective make impact point obvious.

No copy.

### Keyframe
**KF-100 — Pre-impact**

Hands/feet must be anatomically correct.

Bodies should not distort as they approach the surface.

---

## SHOT 07 — Impact

**Narrative function:** Turn the family moment into the website's transition mechanism.

**Approx. source time:** 8.8–9.8 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 76–84%  
**Camera:** Forward motion increases slightly.  
**Family:** Enters water.  
**Water:** Large believable splash propagates toward lens.

### Direction
The splash should be dramatic enough to obscure the scene, but physically plausible.

Avoid a stylized explosion of water.

### Keyframes
**KF-108 — First contact**  
**KF-112 — Splash expansion**  
**KF-116 — Water dominates frame**

KF-116 becomes the visual bridge for hiding any generative seam.

---

## SHOT 08 — Splash wipe

**Narrative function:** Hide the environment transition.

**Approx. source time:** 9.8–10.8 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 84–91%  
**Camera:** Continues through the splash.  
**Frame:** Mostly water, droplets, light, and motion blur.

This shot is technically important because it creates a safe edit zone.

The sequence can switch from the above-water generation to a separately controlled underwater generation while the viewport is visually obscured.

### Keyframe
**KF-120 — Full-screen water transition**

This frame should contain almost no architectural information.

It is the seam-hiding frame.

---

## SHOT 09 — Surface crossing

**Narrative function:** Create the illusion that the visitor entered the pool.

**Approx. source time:** 10.8–11.8 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 91–95%  
**Camera:** Passes through the surface line.  
**Visual:** Refracted light, bubbles, distortion.  
**Environment:** Same pool geometry must remain recognizable.

### Direction
This is the most important continuity test after the family.

The underwater steps, walls, tile, spa, and geometry must correspond to what existed above water.

### Keyframe
**KF-126 — Half above / half below water**

If achievable cleanly, this can be a spectacular frame.

If not, use the splash as a harder concealment and begin fully underwater.

---

## SHOT 10 — Underwater reveal

**Narrative function:** Shift from emotion to craftsmanship.

**Approx. source time:** 11.8–13.5 s  
**Approx. scroll:** 95–100%  
**Camera:** Slow glide underwater, slightly upward or laterally.  
**Family:** Optional distant legs/bubbles only; they should no longer dominate.  
**Pool:** Craftsmanship becomes hero.

### Visual focus
Reveal:
- tile
- lighting
- ledge geometry
- steps
- spa wall or spillway geometry
- clean finish
- underwater light quality

### Copy
Preferred:

> **CRAFTED ABOVE. ENGINEERED BELOW.**

Alternative:

> **CUSTOM POOLS. BUILT DIFFERENT.**

Only one.

### Keyframe
**KF-140 — Underwater craftsmanship hero**

This becomes the transition point into the next homepage section or next cinematic sequence.

# 7. Scroll choreography

Initial prototype mapping:

| Scroll | Beat |
| --- | --- |
| 0–10% | Stillness / opening message |
| 10–23% | Anticipation |
| 23–40% | Run |
| 40–50% | Takeoff |
| 50–68% | Hero flight / controlled slow motion |
| 68–76% | Descent |
| 76–84% | Impact |
| 84–91% | Splash wipe |
| 91–95% | Surface crossing |
| 95–100% | Underwater reveal |

The hero-flight section intentionally receives disproportionate scroll distance.

That is the portion users should want to scrub back and forth.

---

# 8. Motion feel

The scroll mapping should not be perfectly linear.

Recommended conceptual easing:

- establish: slow
- anticipation: medium
- run: accelerate
- takeoff: decelerate
- hero flight: slowest
- descent: accelerate slightly
- impact: quick
- splash wipe: quick
- underwater reveal: slow again

The user's wheel/touch input should remain directly responsive, but frame distribution can create apparent slow motion.

Avoid artificial lag between scroll and frame response.

---

# 9. Copy beats

V1 should use no more than three primary copy moments.

## Beat A — Opening

> **BUILD THE BACKYARD THEY'LL REMEMBER.**

Purpose: emotional premise.

## Beat B — Optional airborne line

> **THIS IS WHY WE BUILD.**

Purpose: reinforce meaning.

Default recommendation: prototype with it, but remove if it competes with the hero image.

## Beat C — Underwater

> **CRAFTED ABOVE. ENGINEERED BELOW.**

Purpose: bridge lifestyle into craftsmanship.

No body paragraphs inside the cinematic sequence.

---

# 10. Sound

V1 should not depend on sound.

The experience must work muted.

Optional later enhancement:
- subtle water ambience
- soft low-frequency cinematic rise
- impact / splash sound
- underwater muffling transition

If added later:
- never autoplay loud audio
- respect mute state
- do not make the experience feel like a video ad

---

# 11. Mobile adaptation

Do not simply crop the desktop sequence.

The mobile sequence should be reframed for portrait composition.

Recommended mobile principles:

- keep father and sons large enough to read
- simplify architecture in background
- preserve pool edge / water as primary spatial anchor
- reduce lateral camera motion
- potentially use fewer frames
- preserve jump, splash, and underwater transitions
- opening copy should fit in 2–3 lines maximum

Mobile is allowed to use a separate crop or separately generated sequence if necessary for quality.

---

# 12. Reduced-motion fallback

For users with `prefers-reduced-motion`:

Use:
- KF-001 as opening image
- a restrained fade/crossfade into KF-080
- then KF-140 as craftsmanship state

No scroll-scrub animation required.

The emotional story should still be understandable.

# 13. Generation strategy

Do not attempt full sequence generation in one uncontrolled prompt.

Use a staged generation pipeline.

## Stage 1 — Canonical hero still
Generate and approve KF-001.

Nothing proceeds until this is right.

## Stage 2 — Character/environment reference pack
Generate a small set of controlled reference stills:
- father front/3-quarter
- sons
- full family
- pool geometry
- backyard wide
- underwater pool geometry

These are continuity assets, not web assets.

## Stage 3 — Above-water motion
Generate Shots 01–07 as one or two controlled motion clips.

Primary continuity target:
- family
- architecture
- pool geometry

## Stage 4 — Splash transition
Generate / refine splash wipe separately if needed.

## Stage 5 — Underwater motion
Generate Shots 09–10 from pool/environment reference.

Primary continuity target:
- exact pool geometry
- material palette
- lighting direction

## Stage 6 — Composite
Use splash frames as edit cover between above-water and underwater clips.

## Stage 7 — Frame extraction
Extract only useful frames after motion quality is approved.

## Stage 8 — Web optimization
Create desktop/tablet/mobile asset sets.

# 15. Acceptance criteria for storyboard execution

The sequence is ready for web implementation only when all are true:

- family identity is consistent across all visible frames
- father and sons remain anatomically credible throughout the jump
- clothing does not change
- home/pool geometry does not visibly morph
- light direction is stable
- pool is visually premium and believable
- jump motion connects physically from final stride to takeoff
- hero-flight silhouettes remain readable
- splash provides sufficient full-frame occlusion to hide an edit
- underwater pool resembles the same pool
- no obvious AI warping at frame-to-frame viewing speed
- KF-001, KF-080, and KF-140 each work as strong standalone stills
- reverse frame playback does not expose severe discontinuities
